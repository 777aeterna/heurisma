Emergence in Physics: A Falsification Test of Collective Order, Universality and Coarse-Graining in Local-Rule Spin Lattices

As-Of Date: 2026-11-24 | Depth Level: Level 3 (Deep Multi-Disciplinary) | Primary Disciplines: Natural & Physical Sciences (statistical and condensed-matter physics) + Formal Sciences (exact solutions, computability) + Humanities (philosophy of science)

Abstract

Simulations of purely local nearest-neighbour spin rules on two different two-dimensional lattices produce a collective, size-dependent critical signature whose exponent matches the exact two-dimensional Ising value on both lattices (1.734 and 1.729 against the exact value), a property that belongs to the lattice as a whole and to no single spin [1] [moderate]. That such macroscopic exponents are universal, i.e. independent of microscopic lattice details, is an established result in the literature [2] [strong]. At the simulated sizes, however, our data cannot pin the transition temperature to within 2 %, cannot decide whether the one-dimensional control lacks a finite-temperature transition, and show that the chosen block-spin observable is not scale-invariant at T_c, so the sealed H₁ is supported only on its exponent clause [1] [preliminary].

Falsifier: Larger-L simulations (L ≥ 64) in which the square- and triangular-lattice exponents separate by more than the minimum detectable difference, or in which the fitted exponent leaves the interval around 7/4.

1. Executive Summary

  • What Would Change This Conclusion: Larger-L simulations (L ≥ 64) in which the square- and triangular-lattice exponents separate by more than the minimum detectable difference, or in which the fitted exponent leaves the interval around 7/4.
  • Key Takeaways:
    • Universality — identical macroscopic exponents on microscopically different lattices — is the physically sharp content of "more is different" [2] [strong]; our two-lattice comparison is consistent with it but could only detect differences larger than 0.195 [1] [moderate].
    • Binder-cumulant crossings of the largest sizes fall close to the exact critical temperatures, but replica-bootstrap errors that re-select the crossing leave every pairwise ±2 % decision undecided [1] [preliminary].
    • The emergence at stake is of Butterfield's kind — novel and robust behaviour that is still deducible in a limit — and not Bedau's "weak emergence", because the 2D Ising transition is derivable analytically, not only by simulation [3], [4] [preliminary].
    • Undecidability theorems show that derivability of macroscopic properties from microscopic rules cannot be assumed in general [5] [strong].

2. Framing & Hypotheses

  • Scientific Discipline(s) & Depth Level: Natural & Physical Sciences (statistical mechanics of phase transitions, renormalization group) governs which macroscopic signatures count as emergent and how to measure them; Formal Sciences (exact solutions and computability theory) governs what can in principle be derived from microscopic rules; Humanities (philosophy of science) governs the definitions of emergence and reduction. Level 3. Coupling: the physical definition of a phase transition requires an infinite-size limit, while the philosophical claim that emergence is "novel and robust behaviour" must be tested in finite systems; computability results constrain any general claim that macroscopic behaviour is always derivable.
  • Research Question: In nearest-neighbour ferromagnetic Ising models (J = 1, k_B = 1) updated by purely local rules, do macroscopic observables (order parameter, susceptibility, Binder cumulant, block-spin correlations) show collective critical behaviour that (a) occurs at a finite temperature only in two dimensions, (b) is quantitatively the same on two different lattices, and (c) is reproduced by simulation of the microscopic rule alone, measured on linear sizes L = 8–32 (2D) and matched chain lengths (1D) as of 2026-11?
  • Hypotheses: (wording exactly as sealed in appendix/preregistration.json; the triangular exact value attributed to "Houtappel" in the sealed text could not be found printed in any retrievable full text, see Section 3)
    • H₀ (no emergent collective singularity / finite-size artifact): in nearest-neighbour Ising models with purely local update rules, the 2D lattices behave like the 1D control — the Binder cumulant curves for different linear sizes L do not intersect at a common temperature within Monte Carlo error, the finite-size susceptibility peak grows with an exponent indistinguishable from the 1D control, and the fitted scaling exponent differs between the square and triangular lattices (no microscopic-detail independence).
    • H₁ (emergent, universal collective order): on both 2D lattices the Binder cumulant curves for different L intersect near the exact critical temperature (Onsager for the square lattice, Houtappel for the triangular lattice) with a relative error below 2 percent, the peak susceptibility grows as a power of L whose exponent is consistent with the exact 2D Ising value of gamma/nu within twice its standard error on both lattices (universality), and the 1D control shows no common crossing and a markedly smaller growth exponent; majority-rule block-spin coarse-graining drives the nearest-neighbour block correlation toward ordered (T<Tc) or disordered (T>Tc) fixed points while it stays approximately scale-invariant near Tc.
    • H₂ (weak vs strong emergence): every macroscopic signature tested is derivable from, and reproduced by, simulation of the microscopic rule alone (weak emergence); refuted if any tested macroscopic regularity cannot be reproduced from the microscopic dynamics in the simulation.
  • Refutation Criteria: The sealed H₁ itself fixes two thresholds: a relative error below 2 % for the Binder crossings and agreement with γ/ν = 7/4 within twice the standard error. We apply them as sealed (2·SE) and, as a disclosed deviation, also with Student t critical values; a crossing passes only if its whole interval lies within ±2 % of T_c, fails if the whole interval lies outside, and is otherwise undecided, and every size pair is judged. Universality is "not rejected" when the square–triangular slope difference lies within the interval (not an equivalence test). The 1D clause and the RG clause have no sealed thresholds and are judged qualitatively. H₂ as sealed cannot fail in this design (Section 4.4) and is treated as a conceptual reading.

4. Findings

4.1 Binder crossings: close to the exact T_c, but not decisively within 2 %

The Binder cumulant is a dimensionless ratio of moments of the order parameter; at a continuous transition its curves for different sizes intersect at the critical temperature. The crossings of the largest pair land close to the exact values on both lattices (+0.06 % square, −0.13 % triangular), but the bootstrap errors are large, and the steepest crossing jumps to another grid interval in a sizeable share of resamples; under both the t-rule and the sealed 2·SE rule every pair is undecided, and smaller pairs cross several percent above T_c [1] [preliminary]. The triangular comparison is additionally weakened because no retrieved full text prints the exact triangular value [8] [preliminary].

Under the post-hoc bracket rule, 4 of 4 square pairs and 4 of 4 triangular pairs show a bracketed crossing, against 1 of 4 for the chain; under the adjacent-point rule no pair on any lattice passes, so that rule does not discriminate [1] [preliminary].

The 1D chain orders completely as T → 0 (U₄ = 2/3 at the lowest grid temperature for every length) and its susceptibility peak grows without bound, the signature of its zero-temperature critical point. Whether the chain lacks a finite-temperature transition cannot be decided from these data: extrapolating its peak temperature gives −0.036 ± 0.020 against 1/ln N but 0.260 ± 0.008 against 1/√N, with equal residuals (1.92×10−4 vs 1.95×10−4), and the 1/ln N form already assumes T_c = 0 [1] [preliminary]. On the 2D lattices the exploratory 1/L extrapolations stay finite (2.339 ± 0.025 and 3.749 ± 0.069) but overshoot T_c, as expected for the |m|-based susceptibility at small L [1] [preliminary].

The order parameter's steepest slope grows with size (square: 1.354 at L = 8 to 2.512 at L = 32), but its growth exponent (0.488 ± 0.054) is far below the finite-size-scaling value 0.875 (z = −7.2), almost certainly because a finite difference on a fixed temperature grid flattens the sharpest curves; we show the figure for illustration only and do not use the statistic as evidence [1] [speculative].

4.2 Universality: the same critical exponent on different microscopic lattices

At a continuous transition the susceptibility peak grows as Lγ/ν; the exact 2D Ising value is γ/ν = 7/4 for every lattice in the universality class, including the square and triangular lattices [2] [strong]. Our fits agree with this value on both lattices under the sealed 2·SE rule and under the df-matched t rule [1] [moderate].

4.3 Coarse-graining: renormalization-group flow of block spins

Majority-rule 3 × 3 blocking implements Kadanoff's block-spin idea, which Wilson turned into the renormalization group: microscopic details are washed out under repeated coarse-graining and only a few relevant parameters survive [10] [strong]. Below T_c the nearest-neighbour block correlation flows toward its ordered limit and above T_c toward zero; at T_c it rises by a statistically clear amount [1] [moderate].

The row at the largest-pair crossing temperature is not an independent control, because that temperature lies within 0.1 % of T_c.

4.4 Which notion of emergence fits

Every signature in Sections 4.1–4.3 was produced by a program containing only the local rule and a random-number generator; as sealed, H₂ could not have failed in this design, so it is a conceptual reading, not a test [1] [preliminary]. Bedau calls a macrostate weakly emergent when it is derivable from micro facts only by simulation [4] [moderate]. The 2D Ising transition does not meet that definition, because its free energy, critical temperature and exponents are derivable analytically [7], [2] [strong]. The definition that fits is Butterfield's: emergence as behaviour that is novel and robust relative to a comparison class, compatible with reduction understood as deduction in a limit [3] [moderate]. Strong emergence, i.e. macroscopic behaviour not fixed by the microscopic dynamics, is neither supported nor excluded by this design [11] [preliminary].

4.5 Robustness of the decisions

Splitting the replicas into two independent seed sets of six (t with 5 degrees of freedom): the exponent stays within its interval around 7/4 on both lattices in both halves (square z = −0.98 and −1.45; triangular z = −0.46 and −0.22), the bracket counts are 3 and 4 (square) and 3 and 4 (triangular) against 0 and 0 for the chain, and the T_c precision claim is not reproduced (undecided / undecided square, undecided / pass triangular) [1] [preliminary]. The integrated autocorrelation time of |m| at the grid point nearest T_c rises from 0.57 to 0.91 sweeps between L = 8 and L = 32 on the square lattice, close to the estimator's floor of one half, and 1, 5 and 5 of 105 points per lattice show a first-half versus second-half drift beyond t·SE [6], [1] [preliminary].

5. Counter-Evidence & Alternative Explanations

  • The infinite limit as the true source of novelty: a strict phase transition exists only for infinitely many spins, so one can argue that the novelty lives in an idealization. Lavis and co-authors argue that large but finite systems already display the relevant behaviour and that the infinite limit is a representational tool; the growth of our susceptibility peaks with L fits that reading [12], [1] [moderate].
  • Undecidable macroscopic properties: for a family of translation-invariant 2D quantum spin lattices, whether the Hamiltonian is gapped or gapless is undecidable, even given a complete microscopic description [5] [strong]. This does not contradict our result for the Ising class, whose behaviour is computable, but it shows that derivability from the micro-rule cannot be assumed for all macroscopic properties [5] [moderate].
  • Protectorates and the limits of reduction: Laughlin and Pines argue that many macroscopic laws are "protected" and cannot in practice be deduced from fundamental equations, and Anderson's "more is different" makes the parallel point about broken symmetry [13], [14] [preliminary]; we read only their abstracts and use them as framing positions, not as evidence.
  • Finite-size, grid and algorithmic artifacts: L ≤ 32 and a fixed temperature grid limit the precision of crossings and make the steepest crossing unstable across resamples; these limits, not the absence of a transition, explain the undecided T_c decisions [1] [moderate].

7. Limitations & Open Questions

  • Verified Methodological & Data Limitations: (1) Sizes are small (L ≤ 32): bootstrap errors of the largest-pair crossings are 0.0445 (square) and 0.1496 (triangular), and pair crossings spread over 3.6 % and 5.2 % of T_c. (2) Robustness rules, the crossing selection and several diagnostics were fixed after the data were seen (Section 3). (3) Seven sources (C1, C2, C5, C6, C9, C11 and the uncited Selke paper) could be read only as abstracts or not at all, and the exact triangular T_c, Ising 1925 and Binder 1981 could not be retrieved in full text. The online searches and the local inventory are logged in appendix/data_attempts.csv, and the dependent claims are tagged [preliminary]. (4) The test covers one universality class and cannot discriminate weak from strong emergence.
  • Actionable Next Steps / Experiments: Extend to L = 64–128 with histogram reweighting and a fitted correction term; judge the 1D clause by an axis-independent criterion such as U₄ at fixed T as N grows; replace nearest-neighbour block correlation by a fitted renormalized coupling to locate the block-spin fixed point; and design an emergence test with an external benchmark that can fail, e.g. predicting a critical temperature before an independent determination.

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- lattices
def lattice(kind: str, L: int) -> tuple[list[tuple[int, ...]], list[tuple[int, ...]]]:
    """Return (all neighbours, forward neighbours) for each site; forward lists count every bond once."""
    if kind == "chain":
        return [((i + 1) % L, (i - 1) % L) for i in range(L)], [((i + 1) % L,) for i in range(L)]
    nbr, fwd = [], []
    for i in range(L * L):
        x, y = i % L, i // L

        def site(dx: int, dy: int) -> int:
            return ((y + dy) % L) * L + (x + dx) % L

        f = [site(1, 0), site(0, 1)]
        b = [site(-1, 0), site(0, -1)]
        if kind == "triangular":  # square lattice plus one diagonal = triangular topology (coordination 6)
            f.append(site(1, 1))
            b.append(site(-1, -1))
        nbr.append(tuple(f + b))
        fwd.append(tuple(f))
    return nbr, fwd


# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- Wolff Monte Carlo
def wolff_sweep(s: list[int], nbr, p: float, rng: random.Random) -> None:
    """Flip Wolff clusters until at least N spins have been flipped (one 'sweep')."""
    n = len(s)
    rand = rng.random
    flipped = 0
    while flipped < n:
        i0 = rng.randrange(n)
        s0 = s[i0]
        s[i0] = -s0
        stack = [i0]
        c = 1
        while stack:
            i = stack.pop()
            for j in nbr[i]:
                if s[j] == s0 and rand() < p:
                    s[j] = -s0
                    stack.append(j)
                    c += 1
        flipped += c


def tau_int(series: list[float]) -> float:
    """Integrated autocorrelation time (in sweeps) with the automatic window W >= 6 tau (Sokal)."""
    n = len(series)
    mu = statistics.fmean(series)
    dev = [v - mu for v in series]
    c0 = sum(d * d for d in dev) / n
    if c0 <= 0:
        return 0.5
    tau = 0.5
    for t in range(1, n // 2):
        tau += sum(dev[i] * dev[i + t] for i in range(n - t)) / ((n - t) * c0)
        if t >= 6 * tau:
            break
    return max(tau, 0.5)


def simulate(task: tuple[str, int, float, int]) -> tuple[float, ...]:
    kind, L, T, rep = task
    nbr, fwd = lattice(kind, L)
    n = len(nbr)
    rng = random.Random(f"{kind}|{L}|{T:.6f}|{rep}")
    s = [1 if rng.random() < 0.5 else -1 for _ in range(n)]
    p = 1.0 - math.exp(-2.0 / T)
    getters = [itemgetter(*[f[d] for f in fwd]) for d in range(len(fwd[0]))]  # one getter per bond direction
    for _ in range(N_EQ):
        wolff_sweep(s, nbr, p, rng)
    ms, es = [], []
    for _ in range(N_MEAS):
        wolff_sweep(s, nbr, p, rng)
        ms.append(abs(sum(s)) / n)
        es.append(-sum(sum(map(mul, s, g(s))) for g in getters) / n)
    k = float(N_MEAS)
    a1 = sum(ms) / k
    a2 = sum(m * m for m in ms) / k
    a4 = sum(m ** 4 for m in ms) / k
    e1 = sum(es) / k
    e2 = sum(e * e for e in es) / k
    half = N_MEAS // 2
    drift = statistics.fmean(ms[:half]) - statistics.fmean(ms[half:])
    return a1, a2, a4, e1, e2, tau_int(ms), drift


def block_spin_task(task: tuple[int, str, float, int]) -> list[float]:
    """Majority-rule 3x3 blocking of square configurations; returns nn correlation per blocking level."""
    L, tag, T, rep = task
    nbr, _ = lattice("square", L)
    rng = random.Random(f"rg|{L}|{T:.6f}|{rep}")
    s = [1 if rng.random() < 0.5 else -1 for _ in range(L * L)]
    p = 1.0 - math.exp(-2.0 / T)
    for _ in range(RG_EQ):
        wolff_sweep(s, nbr, p, rng)
    n_levels = round(math.log(L, 3))  # 81 -> levels 81, 27, 9, 3 ; 27 -> 27, 9, 3
    acc = [0.0] * n_levels
    n_cfg = RG_CFG
    for _ in range(n_cfg):
        for _ in range(3):
            wolff_sweep(s, nbr, p, rng)
        conf, size = s[:], L
        for level in range(n_levels):
            tot = 0
            for y in range(size):
                row = y * size
                for x in range(size):
                    v = conf[row + x]
                    tot += v * conf[row + (x + 1) % size] + v * conf[((y + 1) % size) * size + x]
            acc[level] += tot / (2.0 * size * size)
            if level < n_levels - 1:
                nsz = size // 3
                new = []
                for by in range(nsz):
                    for bx in range(nsz):
                        t = 0
                        for dy in range(3):
                            base = (3 * by + dy) * size + 3 * bx
                            t += conf[base] + conf[base + 1] + conf[base + 2]
                        new.append(1 if t > 0 else -1)
                conf, size = new, nsz
    return [a / n_cfg for a in acc]


# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- estimators
def derived(moms: list[tuple[float, ...]], n_spins: int, T: float) -> dict[str, float]:
    a1 = statistics.fmean(m[0] for m in moms)
    a2 = statistics.fmean(m[1] for m in moms)
    a4 = statistics.fmean(m[2] for m in moms)
    e1 = statistics.fmean(m[3] for m in moms)
    e2 = statistics.fmean(m[4] for m in moms)
    return {
        "m": a1,
        "chi": n_spins * (a2 - a1 * a1) / T,
        "U": 1.0 - a4 / (3.0 * a2 * a2),
        "C": n_spins * (e2 - e1 * e1) / (T * T),
    }


def peak(ts: list[float], ys: list[float]) -> tuple[float, float, bool]:
    """Parabola through the grid maximum and its neighbours; returns (T_peak, y_peak, interior)."""
    k = max(range(len(ys)), key=lambda i: ys[i])
    if k == 0 or k == len(ys) - 1:
        return ts[k], ys[k], False
    x0, x1, x2 = ts[k - 1], ts[k], ts[k + 1]
    y0, y1, y2 = ys[k - 1], ys[k], ys[k + 1]
    h = x1 - x0
    den = y0 - 2 * y1 + y2
    if den >= 0:
        return x1, y1, True
    d = 0.5 * h * (y0 - y2) / den
    return x1 + d, y1 - 0.25 * (y0 - y2) * d / h, True


def crossings(ts: list[float], ua: list[float], ub: list[float]) -> list[float]:
    """All temperatures where U_b - U_a (b = larger size) changes sign from + to - (linear interpolation)."""
    out = []
    for k in range(len(ts) - 1):
        d0, d1 = ub[k] - ua[k], ub[k + 1] - ua[k + 1]
        if d0 > 0 >= d1:
            out.append(ts[k] + (ts[k + 1] - ts[k]) * d0 / (d0 - d1))
    return out


def main_crossing(ts: list[float], ua: list[float], ub: list[float]) -> float | None:
    """The + to - crossing with the steepest change of U_b - U_a."""
    best, best_slope = None, -1.0
    for k in range(len(ts) - 1):
        d0, d1 = ub[k] - ua[k], ub[k + 1] - ua[k + 1]
        if d0 > 0 >= d1 and d0 - d1 > best_slope:
            best_slope = d0 - d1
            best = ts[k] + (ts[k + 1] - ts[k]) * d0 / (d0 - d1)
    return best


def ols(xs: list[float], ys: list[float]) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
    """Slope, intercept and OLS standard error of the slope."""
    n = len(xs)
    mx, my = statistics.fmean(xs), statistics.fmean(ys)
    sxx = sum((x - mx) ** 2 for x in xs)
    b = sum((x - mx) * (y - my) for x, y in zip(xs, ys)) / sxx
    a = my - b * mx
    se = math.sqrt(sum((y - a - b * x) ** 2 for x, y in zip(xs, ys)) / (n - 2) / sxx) if n > 2 else float("nan")
    return b, a, se


def jackknife_se(loo: list[float]) -> float:
    n = len(loo)
    mean = statistics.fmean(loo)
    return math.sqrt((n - 1) / n * sum((v - mean) ** 2 for v in loo))


def write_json(path: Path, payload: object) -> None:

def analyse(kind: str, sizes: list[int], reps: list[int], results: dict, tcrit: float) -> dict:
    """Curves, peaks, slopes and Binder crossings for one lattice from a subset of replicas (jackknife SEs)."""
    ts = T_GRID[kind]
    full: dict[int, dict[str, list[float]]] = {}
    loo: list[dict[int, dict[str, list[float]]]] = [dict() for _ in reps]
    for L in sizes:
        n_spins = L if kind == "chain" else L * L
        f = {"m": [], "chi": [], "U": [], "C": []}
        lo = [{"m": [], "chi": [], "U": [], "C": []} for _ in reps]
        for T in ts:
            moms = [results[(kind, L, T, r)] for r in reps]
            d = derived(moms, n_spins, T)
            for key in f:
                f[key].append(d[key])
            for q in range(len(reps)):
                dl = derived(moms[:q] + moms[q + 1:], n_spins, T)
                for key in f:
                    lo[q][key].append(dl[key])
        full[L] = f
        for q in range(len(reps)):
            loo[q][L] = lo[q]
    nq = len(reps)
    peaks = {}
    for L in sizes:
        tp, cp, interior = peak(ts, full[L]["chi"])
        peaks[L] = (tp, cp, jackknife_se([peak(ts, loo[q][L]["chi"])[1] for q in range(nq)]), interior)
    lnL = [math.log(L) for L in sizes]
    slope, _, se_ols = ols(lnL, [math.log(peaks[L][1]) for L in sizes])
    se_jk = jackknife_se([ols(lnL, [math.log(peak(ts, loo[q][L]["chi"])[1]) for L in sizes])[0] for q in range(nq)])
    se = max(se_ols, se_jk)
    drift = {}
    for start in range(3):
        sub = sizes[start:]
        b, _, s_ols = ols([math.log(L) for L in sub], [math.log(peaks[L][1]) for L in sub])
        s_jk = jackknife_se([ols([math.log(L) for L in sub], [math.log(peak(ts, loo[q][L]["chi"])[1]) for L in sub])[0]
                             for q in range(nq)])
        drift[sub[0]] = (b, max(s_ols, s_jk))
    pairs = []
    for a, b in zip(sizes[:-1], sizes[1:]):
        allc = crossings(ts, full[a]["U"], full[b]["U"])
        mc = main_crossing(ts, full[a]["U"], full[b]["U"])
        mc_loo = [main_crossing(ts, loo[q][a]["U"], loo[q][b]["U"]) for q in range(nq)]
        mc_se = jackknife_se(mc_loo) if mc is not None and all(v is not None for v in mc_loo) else float("nan")
        adjacent, below, above = [], False, False
        for k in range(len(ts)):
            dk = full[b]["U"][k] - full[a]["U"][k]
            sk = jackknife_se([loo[q][b]["U"][k] - loo[q][a]["U"][k] for q in range(nq)])
            if mc is not None and ts[k] < mc and dk > tcrit * sk:
                below = True
            if mc is not None and ts[k] > mc and dk < -tcrit * sk:
                above = True
            if k < len(ts) - 1:
                d1 = full[b]["U"][k + 1] - full[a]["U"][k + 1]
                s1 = jackknife_se([loo[q][b]["U"][k + 1] - loo[q][a]["U"][k + 1] for q in range(nq)])
                if dk > tcrit * sk and -d1 > tcrit * s1:
                    adjacent.append(ts[k] + (ts[k + 1] - ts[k]) * dk / (dk - d1))
        pairs.append({"a": a, "b": b, "all": allc, "mc": mc, "mc_se": mc_se, "adjacent": adjacent,
                      "bracket": below and above})
    return {"full": full, "peaks": peaks, "slope": slope, "se_ols": se_ols, "se_jk": se_jk, "se": se,
            "drift": drift, "pairs": pairs, "n_bracket": sum(p["bracket"] for p in pairs),
            "n_adjacent": sum(1 for p in pairs if p["adjacent"])}


def tc_decision(est: float | None, se: float, exact: float, tcrit: float) -> str:
    if est is None or math.isnan(se):
        return "undecided (no crossing or no SE)"
    lo, hi = est - tcrit * se, est + tcrit * se
    if lo >= exact * (1 - TOL_TC) and hi <= exact * (1 + TOL_TC):
        return "pass (whole interval within tolerance)"
    if hi < exact * (1 - TOL_TC) or lo > exact * (1 + TOL_TC):
        return "fail (whole interval outside tolerance)"
    return "undecided (interval straddles the tolerance)"


def bootstrap_crossing(kind: str, a: int, b: int, results: dict) -> tuple[float, float, int]:
    """Replica bootstrap of the steepest crossing, re-selecting the crossing in every resample.
    Returns (bootstrap SE, fraction of resamples whose selected crossing lies in a different grid interval, n valid)."""
    ts = T_GRID[kind]
    rng = random.Random(f"boot|{kind}|{a}|{b}")
    def curve(L: int, reps: list[int]) -> list[float]:
        n_spins = L if kind == "chain" else L * L
        return [derived([results[(kind, L, T, r)] for r in reps], n_spins, T)["U"] for T in ts]
    full = list(range(N_REP))
    ref = main_crossing(ts, curve(a, full), curve(b, full))
    vals, moved = [], 0
    for _ in range(N_BOOT):
        reps = [rng.randrange(N_REP) for _ in range(N_REP)]
        c = main_crossing(ts, curve(a, reps), curve(b, reps))
        if c is None:
            moved += 1
            continue
        vals.append(c)
        if ref is not None and abs(c - ref) > (ts[1] - ts[0]):
            moved += 1
    se = statistics.stdev(vals) if len(vals) > 1 else float("nan")
    return se, moved / N_BOOT, len(vals)

        n_axis = 1.0 if kind == "chain" else 2.0
        out(f"slope against ln(number of spins N): {r['slope'] / n_axis:.4f} +/- {r['se'] / n_axis:.4f}")
        num[f"gnuN_{key}"] = f"{r['slope'] / n_axis:.3f}"
        num[f"gnuN_{key}_se"] = f"{r['se'] / n_axis:.3f}"
        if kind != "chain":
            z = (r["slope"] - GAMMA_OVER_NU) / r["se"]
            t_use = T_CRIT_OLS if r["se_ols"] >= r["se_jk"] else T_CRIT
            r["t_use"] = t_use
            out(f"deviation from exact gamma/nu: {r['slope'] - GAMMA_OVER_NU:+.4f} (z = {z:+.2f}; df-matched t = {t_use} -> "
                f"pass {abs(z) <= t_use}; sealed 2*SE rule -> pass {abs(z) <= 2.0})")
            num[f"t_use_{key}"] = f"{t_use:.3f}"
            num[f"gnu_{key}_sealed"] = "pass" if abs(z) <= 2.0 else "fail"
            num[f"z_{key}"] = f"{z:+.2f}"
            r["z"] = z
        num[f"gnu_{key}"] = f"{r['slope']:.3f}"
        num[f"gnu_{key}_se"] = f"{r['se']:.3f}"

        # peak-temperature extrapolation: 2D T_peak = T_inf + a / L (nu = 1); 1D T_peak = T_inf + a / ln N
        xs = [1.0 / L for L in sizes] if kind != "chain" else [1.0 / math.log(L) for L in sizes]
        b, t_inf, s_b = ols(xs, [r["peaks"][L][0] for L in sizes])
        mx = statistics.fmean(xs)
        resid = [r["peaks"][L][0] - t_inf - b * x for L, x in zip(sizes, xs)]
        s_int = math.sqrt(sum(e * e for e in resid) / (len(xs) - 2) * (1.0 / len(xs) + mx * mx / sum((x - mx) ** 2 for x in xs)))
        var = "1/L" if kind != "chain" else "1/ln N"
        out(f"exploratory: susceptibility-peak temperature extrapolated linearly in {var} to infinite size: "
            f"T_inf = {t_inf:.4f} +/- {s_int:.4f} (OLS)")
        num[f"tinf_{key}"] = f"{t_inf:.3f}"
        num[f"tinf_{key}_se"] = f"{s_int:.3f}"
        if kind == "chain":
            for name, fx in (("lnN", lambda L: 1.0 / math.log(L)), ("sqrtN", lambda L: 1.0 / math.sqrt(L)), ("N", lambda L: 1.0 / L)):
                xs2 = [fx(L) for L in sizes]
                ys2 = [r["peaks"][L][0] for L in sizes]
                b2, a2, _ = ols(xs2, ys2)
                m2 = statistics.fmean(xs2)
                rss = sum((y - a2 - b2 * x) ** 2 for x, y in zip(xs2, ys2))
                se2 = math.sqrt(rss / (len(xs2) - 2) * (1.0 / len(xs2) + m2 * m2 / sum((x - m2) ** 2 for x in xs2)))
                out(f"  1D peak-temperature extrapolation against {name}: T_inf = {a2:.4f} +/- {se2:.4f}, residual sum of squares = {rss:.2e}")
                num[f"tinf_ch_{name}"] = f"{a2:.3f}"
                num[f"tinf_ch_{name}_se"] = f"{se2:.3f}"
                num[f"rss_ch_{name}"] = f"{rss:.2e}"

        out(f"Binder cumulant crossings (adjacent sizes; all + to - sign changes of U_large - U_small):")
        for p in r["pairs"]:
            allc = ", ".join(f"{v:.4f}" for v in p["all"]) if p["all"] else "none"
            mc = "none" if p["mc"] is None else (f"{p['mc']:.4f} +/- n/a" if math.isnan(p["mc_se"]) else f"{p['mc']:.4f} +/- {p['mc_se']:.4f}")
            out(f"  ({p['a']},{p['b']}): all crossings = {allc}; steepest = {mc}; "
                f"adjacent-point rule = {'yes' if p['adjacent'] else 'no'}; bracket rule = {p['bracket']}")
            if kind != "chain" and p["mc"] is not None:
                bse, moved, nval = bootstrap_crossing(kind, p["a"], p["b"], results)

                dec_2 = tc_decision(p["mc"], bse, TC_EXACT[kind], 2.0).split(" (")[0]
                relp = 100 * (p["mc"] - TC_EXACT[kind]) / TC_EXACT[kind]

                    f"{100 * moved:.0f} % of resamples); rel. error {relp:+.2f} %; decision with t: {dec_t}; with sealed 2*SE: {dec_2}")

                num[f"cross_{key}_{p['a']}_{p['b']}_rel"] = f"{relp:+.2f}"
                num[f"cross_{key}_{p['a']}_{p['b']}_dec"] = dec_t
                num[f"cross_{key}_{p['a']}_{p['b']}_dec2"] = dec_2

        num[f"nbracket_{key}"] = r["n_bracket"]
        num[f"nadjacent_{key}"] = r["n_adjacent"]

            num["msharp_fss"] = f"{SHARP_EXP_FSS:.3f}"
            kc = min(range(len(ts)), key=lambda k: abs(ts[k] - TC_EXACT[kind]))
            taus_c = [statistics.fmean(results[(kind, L, ts[kc], q)][5] for q in range(N_REP)) for L in sizes]
            out(f"  mean tau_int of |m| at T = {ts[kc]:.4f} (grid point nearest Tc) by size: "

            out(f"  C_max fits: C = a + b ln L with b = {b_log:.3f}, RSS = {rss_log:.2e}; C = a L^x with x = {b_pow:.3f}, RSS = {rss_pow:.2e}")
            num[f"cfit_{key}_b"] = f"{b_log:.3f}"
            num[f"cfit_{key}_rsslog"] = f"{rss_log:.2e}"
            num[f"cfit_{key}_x"] = f"{b_pow:.3f}"
            num[f"cfit_{key}_rsspow"] = f"{rss_pow:.2e}"
            if kind == "triangular":
                kp = max(range(len(ts)), key=lambda k: r["full"][32]["chi"][k])
                chis = []
                for q in range(N_REP):
                    a1, a2 = results[(kind, 32, ts[kp], q)][0], results[(kind, 32, ts[kp], q)][1]

                out(f"  triangular L=32 per-replica chi at grid peak T = {ts[kp]:.4f}: " + ", ".join(f"{v:.2f}" for v in chis))
                med = statistics.median(chis)
                mad = statistics.median(abs(v - med) for v in chis)
                n_out = sum(1 for v in chis if mad > 0 and abs(v - med) > 3 * mad / 0.6745)
                out(f"  replicas beyond median +/- 3 MAD/0.6745: {n_out}")
                num["chi_tri32_nout"] = n_out
        out()

    sq, tr, ch = res["square"], res["triangular"], res["chain"]
    diff = sq["slope"] - tr["slope"]
    diff_se = math.sqrt(sq["se"] ** 2 + tr["se"] ** 2)

    out(f"against ln(N spins): square {sq['slope'] / 2:.4f}, triangular {tr['slope'] / 2:.4f}, 1D {ch['slope']:.4f} "
        f"(the chain's chi_max grows faster per spin; the exponent comparison depends on the size variable)")

                "rg_step81": f"{m81:+.3f}", "rg_step81_se": f"{s81:.3f}", "rg_step27": f"{m27:+.3f}", "rg_step27_se": f"{s27:.3f}",
