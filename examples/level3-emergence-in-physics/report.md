## 1. Executive Summary
- **Calibrated Verdict:** Physical emergence in generic local many-body systems is predominantly **weak and asymptotic**, not a violation of microscopic laws: exact phase-transition singularities occur strictly in the thermodynamic limit (N → ∞), yet finite-N classical lattices already exhibit universal finite-size scaling precursors `[Strong]`. Our 2D Ising simulations across square and triangular lattices recover γ/ν = {{num:gamma_nu_hat}} and {{num:tri_gn_hat}} (exact {{num:gamma_nu_exact}}) and Binder crossings bracketing Onsager Tc = {{num:tc_exact}} within Monte Carlo uncertainty `[Moderate]`. Nevertheless, weak emergence does not guarantee algorithmic derivability: formal proofs establish that the macroscopic spectral gap of local 1D and 2D quantum Hamiltonians is undecidable, bounding micro-to-macro deduction `[Strong]`.
- **What Would Change This Conclusion:** Replicated experimental detection of macroscopic downward causation violating microscopic conservation laws or unitarity would falsify the weak-emergence synthesis; conversely, a proof that undecidable spectral-gap Hamiltonians have measure zero under generic physical perturbations and thermal noise would narrow the practical scope of formal irreducibility.
- **Key Takeaways:**
  - **Finite-N analyticity vs. asymptotic singularities:** Every finite-N local Hamiltonian has a strictly analytic partition function and, under exact ℤ₂ symmetry (zero external field), zero exact signed magnetization (⟨m⟩ = {{num:exact_L4_m_signed}} on L = 4), while collective observables sharpen systematically toward thermodynamic-limit singularities via Lee–Yang zero pinching and renormalization-group (RG) fixed points `[Strong]`.
  - **Universality across microscopic Hamiltonians:** High-precision 3D multi-model lattice benchmarks (`@C1`) establish identical 3D Ising critical exponents across distinct microscopic Hamiltonians `[Strong]`, and our 2D square vs. triangular Ising simulations (`@C11`) yield consistent 2D Ising exponents despite distinct coordination numbers and critical temperatures `[Moderate]`.
  - **Beyond symmetry breaking:** Emergence in quantum matter extends past Landau order parameters to gauge-boson mass generation and long-range entangled topological order (established in fractional quantum Hall states, while bulk quantum spin liquids remain under active experimental investigation) `[Moderate]`.
  - **Formal ceiling without ontological vitalism:** Even with completely known local microscopic rules, deciding whether a 1D or 2D quantum lattice Hamiltonian is gapped in the thermodynamic limit is algorithmically undecidable—breaking the smooth finite-N precursor intuition for adversarial quantum models `[Strong]`.

## 4. Findings

### 4.1 Finite-N Analyticity vs. Thermodynamic-Limit Emergence (Lee–Yang Mechanism)
For any finite system of N spins with bounded local interactions, the canonical partition function Z_N(T) is a finite sum of positive exponential terms and is therefore strictly analytic for all real temperatures T > 0 [@C2] `[Strong]`. By exact ℤ₂ spin-flip symmetry (sᵢ → −sᵢ), the equilibrium signed magnetization vanishes identically on every finite lattice. Exact enumeration at Tc confirms ⟨m⟩ = {{num:exact_L2_m_signed}} (L = 2), {{num:exact_L3_m_signed}} (L = 3), and {{num:exact_L4_m_signed}} (L = 4), demonstrating that exact non-analytic spontaneous symmetry breaking cannot occur at finite N [@C2; @C10; @C11] `[Strong]`.

In the Yang–Lee formalism, equilibrium phase transitions emerge as complex-plane zeros of Z_N accumulate and pinch the real axis strictly in the thermodynamic limit N → ∞—a mechanism established rigorously both for general equilibrium/nonequilibrium transitions [@C2] and specifically for first-order phase transitions [@C7] `[Strong]`. Nevertheless, finite-N classical systems do not remain featureless until N = ∞: as complex zeros approach the real axis with growing L, collective observables exhibit smooth but rapidly sharpening precursors, supporting Butterfield and Bouatta's and Landsman's philosophical thesis that emergent macroscopic novelty is compatible with reduction to finite-N microphysics [@C8; @C10; @C11] `[Moderate]`.

### 4.3 Emergence Beyond Landau Symmetry Breaking: Gauge Masses and Topological Order
Emergence in physics extends beyond classical Landau symmetry breaking in two major directions:
- **Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking in Gauge Theories:** When a continuous local gauge symmetry is spontaneously broken, the would-be Goldstone mode is absorbed to yield massive vector bosons—an emergent mass gap absent from the unbroken microscopic Lagrangian [@C9; @C10] `[Strong]`.
- **Topological Order & Long-Range Entanglement:** Zero-temperature quantum many-body phases can share identical symmetries and lack any local order parameter, instead being classified by global patterns of long-range quantum entanglement (topological order) that give rise to emergent fractionalized excitations (anyons) and emergent gauge fields [@C4] `[Moderate]`. Fractional quantum Hall states provide well-established experimental realizations of topological order, whereas candidate bulk quantum spin liquids remain under active experimental investigation [@C4] `[Moderate]`.

### 4.4 Formal Irreducibility: Algorithmic Undecidability of the Spectral Gap (Testing H₂)
While H₁ accounts for universality and finite-size scaling in generic classical models without violating microscopic physics, formal mathematical physics proves a sharp ceiling on algorithmic reducibility. Cubitt, Perez-Garcia, and Wolf proved that deciding whether a translationally invariant, nearest-neighbor 2D quantum spin Hamiltonian has a gapped or gapless spectrum in the thermodynamic limit is **algorithmically undecidable** (reducible to the Halting Problem) [@C5] `[Strong]`. Bausch et al. proved that this undecidability persists even in **one-dimensional** translation-invariant local spin chains [@C6] `[Strong]`.

This establishes **formal irreducibility without strong ontological emergence**: no microscopic conservation law is violated, yet no general algorithm can infer the thermodynamic phase from finite-size observations because an undecidable Hamiltonian can mimic a gapless or gapped phase up to an uncomputably large lattice size before abruptly switching phase [@C5; @C6] `[Strong]`.

## 5. Counter-Evidence & Alternative Explanations
- **Cross-Domain Tension — Finite-N Precursors vs. Undecidable Switching:** The reductive reconciliation of Butterfield and Bouatta [@C8] and Landsman [@C10] holds that macroscopic phase behavior is quantitatively realized "on the way to the limit" at large finite N, as seen in our classical 2D Ising Binder crossings (3-seed range [{{num:u4_seed_min}}, {{num:u4_seed_max}}] bracketing exact {{num:tc_exact}}) [@C11] `[Moderate]`. However, this finite-N precursor principle applies strictly to generic, RG-fixed-point models with well-behaved finite-size scaling; it directly conflicts with the undecidable zero-temperature quantum Hamiltonians of Cubitt et al. [@C5] and Bausch et al. [@C6], for which finite-N observation at any computable lattice size provides no guarantee of the N → ∞ phase `[Strong]`.
- **Strong Ontological Emergence & Downward Causation (Exploratory Contrast):** A competing philosophical tradition argues that macroscopic boundary conditions and higher-level constraints exert irreducible "top-down" causation on microscopic constituents (note that open-access full texts for Batterman's singular-limit critique, Ellis 2020, and Juarrero 2023 could not be retrieved in full via OpenAlex/arXiv; see `appendix/data_attempts.csv`). Within physics, while broken-symmetry environments and "flea" perturbations select symmetry-broken sectors in quantum systems [@C10], no Tier 1 experiment has demonstrated a violation of underlying microscopic conservation laws or unitarity, leaving strong ontological emergence unestablished compared with contextual effective-field-theory decoupling [@C3; @C10] `[Moderate]`.
- **Physical Typicality of Spectral-Gap Undecidability:** The undecidable constructions of Cubitt et al. [@C5] and Bausch et al. [@C6] require high local Hilbert-space dimension and fine-tuned Turing-machine encodings. Whether undecidability survives generic disorder, low local dimension, and non-zero temperature in laboratory condensed matter remains open, bounding the empirical—though not the formal—reach of H₂ [@C5; @C6] `[Preliminary]`.

## 7. Limitations & Open Questions
- **Verified Methodological & Data Limitations:**
  - *Simulation Scope, Autocorrelation & Grid Resolution:* Our reproducible Wolff Monte Carlo simulation (`appendix/analysis.py`) spans L = 8 to L = 32 on 2D classical square and triangular lattices without correction-to-scaling terms or continuous histogram reweighting; discrete temperature spacing still yields tied `T_peak` bins between adjacent sizes (`L = 12, 16` at 2.42 and `L = 24, 32` at 2.35), the `log(chi_max)` block-bootstrap conditions on fixed full-sample `T_peak(L)` rather than re-maximizing over T per replicate, the lag-1 block autocorrelation r₁(m²) = {{num:r1_tc_32}} across B = {{num:n_blocks}} blocks at (L = 32, Tc) indicates residual inter-block dependence that may bias naive delete-1 jackknife SEs downward, and the logarithmic specific-heat slope ({{num:cv_log_slope}} ± {{num:cv_log_se}}) retains finite-size corrections relative to the asymptotic Onsager value ({{num:cv_theory_slope}}).
  - *Experimental Datasets & Full-Text Literature Access:* Both online (OpenAlex) and local (`local_data.py inventory`) searches for raw downloadable 2D Ising calorimetry/magnetometry datasets returned no primary CSV series (`appendix/data_attempts.csv`). Likewise, fallback searches for Anderson (1972), Laughlin & Pines (2000), Batterman, Ellis (2020), and Juarrero (2023) yielded no open-access full text or missing database abstracts (`appendix/data_attempts.csv`), so they were excluded rather than cited from incomplete metadata; all ten external citations (`C1`–`C10`) were verified in full text.
  - *Physical Scope of Undecidability:* Existing spectral-gap undecidability proofs (`@C5`, `@C6`) apply to zero-temperature, fine-tuned, high-local-dimension Hamiltonians; their stability under thermal decoherence and generic disorder remains unestablished.
- **Actionable Next Steps / Experiments:**
  - Extend the finite-size scaling benchmark to L = 64–256 with Ferrenberg–Swendsen multi-histogram reweighting to resolve continuous `T_peak(L)` shifts and extract the correlation-length exponent ν independently.
  - Incorporate an open primary experimental calorimetry dataset (e.g., quasi-2D Ising antiferromagnet Rb₂CoF₄ or K₂CoF₄ heat-capacity measurements digitized from archival tables) to test finite-size rounding directly against laboratory data.
  - Investigate whether spectral-gap undecidability persists in low-local-dimension, physically generic quantum Hamiltonians at finite temperature and under realistic decoherence.
