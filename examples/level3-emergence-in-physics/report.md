# Emergence in Physics: A Falsification Test of Collective Order, Universality and Coarse-Graining in Local-Rule Spin Lattices

> **As-Of Date:** 2026-11-24 | **Depth Level:** Level 3 (Deep Multi-Disciplinary) | **Primary Disciplines:** Natural & Physical Sciences (statistical and condensed-matter physics) + Formal Sciences (exact solutions, computability) + Humanities (philosophy of science)

## 1. Executive Summary
- **Calibrated Verdict:** Simulations of purely local nearest-neighbour spin rules on two different two-dimensional lattices produce a collective, size-dependent critical signature whose exponent matches the exact two-dimensional Ising value on both lattices ({{num:gnu_sq}} and {{num:gnu_tri}} against the exact value), a property that belongs to the lattice as a whole and to no single spin [@C12] `[Moderate]`. That such macroscopic exponents are universal, i.e. independent of microscopic lattice details, is an established result in the literature [@C3] `[Strong]`. At the simulated sizes, however, our data cannot pin the transition temperature to within 2 %, cannot decide whether the one-dimensional control lacks a finite-temperature transition, and show that the chosen block-spin observable is not scale-invariant at T_c, so the sealed H₁ is supported only on its exponent clause [@C12] `[Preliminary]`.
- **What Would Change This Conclusion:** Larger-L simulations (L ≥ 64) in which the square- and triangular-lattice exponents separate by more than the minimum detectable difference, or in which the fitted exponent leaves the interval around 7/4.
- **Key Takeaways:**
  - Universality — identical macroscopic exponents on microscopically different lattices — is the physically sharp content of "more is different" [@C3] `[Strong]`; our two-lattice comparison is consistent with it but could only detect differences larger than {{num:mdd_sq_tri}} [@C12] `[Moderate]`.
  - Binder-cumulant crossings of the largest sizes fall close to the exact critical temperatures, but replica-bootstrap errors that re-select the crossing leave every pairwise ±2 % decision undecided [@C12] `[Preliminary]`.
  - The emergence at stake is of Butterfield's kind — novel and robust behaviour that is still deducible in a limit — and not Bedau's "weak emergence", because the 2D Ising transition is derivable analytically, not only by simulation [@C7; @C13] `[Preliminary]`.
  - Undecidability theorems show that derivability of macroscopic properties from microscopic rules cannot be assumed in general [@C8] `[Strong]`.

## 2. Framing & Hypotheses
- **Scientific Discipline(s) & Depth Level:** Natural & Physical Sciences (statistical mechanics of phase transitions, renormalization group) governs which macroscopic signatures count as emergent and how to measure them; Formal Sciences (exact solutions and computability theory) governs what can in principle be derived from microscopic rules; Humanities (philosophy of science) governs the definitions of emergence and reduction. **Level 3.** Coupling: the physical definition of a phase transition requires an infinite-size limit, while the philosophical claim that emergence is "novel and robust behaviour" must be tested in finite systems; computability results constrain any general claim that macroscopic behaviour is always derivable.
- **Research Question:** In nearest-neighbour ferromagnetic Ising models (J = 1, k_B = 1) updated by purely local rules, do macroscopic observables (order parameter, susceptibility, Binder cumulant, block-spin correlations) show collective critical behaviour that (a) occurs at a finite temperature only in two dimensions, (b) is quantitatively the same on two different lattices, and (c) is reproduced by simulation of the microscopic rule alone, measured on linear sizes L = 8–32 (2D) and matched chain lengths (1D) as of 2026-11?
- **Hypotheses:** (wording exactly as sealed in `appendix/preregistration.json`; the triangular exact value attributed to "Houtappel" in the sealed text could not be found printed in any retrievable full text, see Section 3)
  - H₀ (no emergent collective singularity / finite-size artifact): in nearest-neighbour Ising models with purely local update rules, the 2D lattices behave like the 1D control — the Binder cumulant curves for different linear sizes L do not intersect at a common temperature within Monte Carlo error, the finite-size susceptibility peak grows with an exponent indistinguishable from the 1D control, and the fitted scaling exponent differs between the square and triangular lattices (no microscopic-detail independence).
  - H₁ (emergent, universal collective order): on both 2D lattices the Binder cumulant curves for different L intersect near the exact critical temperature (Onsager for the square lattice, Houtappel for the triangular lattice) with a relative error below 2 percent, the peak susceptibility grows as a power of L whose exponent is consistent with the exact 2D Ising value of gamma/nu within twice its standard error on both lattices (universality), and the 1D control shows no common crossing and a markedly smaller growth exponent; majority-rule block-spin coarse-graining drives the nearest-neighbour block correlation toward ordered (T<Tc) or disordered (T>Tc) fixed points while it stays approximately scale-invariant near Tc.
  - H₂ (weak vs strong emergence): every macroscopic signature tested is derivable from, and reproduced by, simulation of the microscopic rule alone (weak emergence); refuted if any tested macroscopic regularity cannot be reproduced from the microscopic dynamics in the simulation.
- **Refutation Criteria:** The sealed H₁ itself fixes two thresholds: a relative error below 2 % for the Binder crossings and agreement with γ/ν = 7/4 within twice the standard error. We apply them as sealed (2·SE) and, as a disclosed deviation, also with Student t critical values; a crossing passes only if its whole interval lies within ±2 % of T_c, fails if the whole interval lies outside, and is otherwise undecided, and every size pair is judged. Universality is "not rejected" when the square–triangular slope difference lies within the interval (not an equivalence test). The 1D clause and the RG clause have no sealed thresholds and are judged qualitatively. H₂ as sealed cannot fail in this design (Section 4.4) and is treated as a conceptual reading.

## 4. Findings

### 4.1 Binder crossings: close to the exact T_c, but not decisively within 2 %
The Binder cumulant is a dimensionless ratio of moments of the order parameter; at a continuous transition its curves for different sizes intersect at the critical temperature. The crossings of the largest pair land close to the exact values on both lattices ({{num:tc_sq_rel}} % square, {{num:tc_tri_rel}} % triangular), but the bootstrap errors are large, and the steepest crossing jumps to another grid interval in a sizeable share of resamples; under both the t-rule and the sealed 2·SE rule every pair is undecided, and smaller pairs cross several percent above T_c [@C12] `[Preliminary]`. The triangular comparison is additionally weakened because no retrieved full text prints the exact triangular value [@C2] `[Preliminary]`.

### 4.3 Coarse-graining: renormalization-group flow of block spins
Majority-rule 3 × 3 blocking implements Kadanoff's block-spin idea, which Wilson turned into the renormalization group: microscopic details are washed out under repeated coarse-graining and only a few relevant parameters survive [@C4] `[Strong]`. Below T_c the nearest-neighbour block correlation flows toward its ordered limit and above T_c toward zero; at T_c it rises by a statistically clear amount [@C12] `[Moderate]`.

### 4.4 Which notion of emergence fits
Every signature in Sections 4.1–4.3 was produced by a program containing only the local rule and a random-number generator; as sealed, H₂ could not have failed in this design, so it is a conceptual reading, not a test [@C12] `[Preliminary]`. Bedau calls a macrostate weakly emergent when it is derivable from micro facts *only* by simulation [@C13] `[Moderate]`. The 2D Ising transition does not meet that definition, because its free energy, critical temperature and exponents are derivable analytically [@C1; @C3] `[Strong]`. The definition that fits is Butterfield's: emergence as behaviour that is novel and robust relative to a comparison class, compatible with reduction understood as deduction in a limit [@C7] `[Moderate]`. Strong emergence, i.e. macroscopic behaviour not fixed by the microscopic dynamics, is neither supported nor excluded by this design [@C11] `[Preliminary]`.

### 4.5 Robustness of the decisions
Splitting the replicas into two independent seed sets of six (t with 5 degrees of freedom): the exponent stays within its interval around 7/4 on both lattices in both halves (square z = {{num:halfA_sq_z}} and {{num:halfB_sq_z}}; triangular z = {{num:halfA_tri_z}} and {{num:halfB_tri_z}}), the bracket counts are {{num:halfA_sq_nbracket}} and {{num:halfB_sq_nbracket}} (square) and {{num:halfA_tri_nbracket}} and {{num:halfB_tri_nbracket}} (triangular) against {{num:halfA_ch_nbracket}} and {{num:halfB_ch_nbracket}} for the chain, and the T_c precision claim is not reproduced ({{num:halfA_sq_dec}} / {{num:halfB_sq_dec}} square, {{num:halfA_tri_dec}} / {{num:halfB_tri_dec}} triangular) [@C12] `[Preliminary]`. The integrated autocorrelation time of |m| at the grid point nearest T_c rises from {{num:tauc_sq_8}} to {{num:tauc_sq_32}} sweeps between L = 8 and L = 32 on the square lattice, close to the estimator's floor of one half, and {{num:ndrift_sq}}, {{num:ndrift_tri}} and {{num:ndrift_ch}} of {{num:npts_sq}} points per lattice show a first-half versus second-half drift beyond t·SE [@C5; @C12] `[Preliminary]`.

## 5. Counter-Evidence & Alternative Explanations
- **The infinite limit as the true source of novelty:** a strict phase transition exists only for infinitely many spins, so one can argue that the novelty lives in an idealization. Lavis and co-authors argue that large but finite systems already display the relevant behaviour and that the infinite limit is a representational tool; the growth of our susceptibility peaks with L fits that reading [@C10; @C12] `[Moderate]`.
- **Undecidable macroscopic properties:** for a family of translation-invariant 2D quantum spin lattices, whether the Hamiltonian is gapped or gapless is undecidable, even given a complete microscopic description [@C8] `[Strong]`. This does not contradict our result for the Ising class, whose behaviour is computable, but it shows that derivability from the micro-rule cannot be assumed for all macroscopic properties [@C8] `[Moderate]`.
- **Protectorates and the limits of reduction:** Laughlin and Pines argue that many macroscopic laws are "protected" and cannot in practice be deduced from fundamental equations, and Anderson's "more is different" makes the parallel point about broken symmetry [@C9; @C6] `[Preliminary]`; we read only their abstracts and use them as framing positions, not as evidence.
- **Finite-size, grid and algorithmic artifacts:** L ≤ 32 and a fixed temperature grid limit the precision of crossings and make the steepest crossing unstable across resamples; these limits, not the absence of a transition, explain the undecided T_c decisions [@C12] `[Moderate]`.

## 7. Limitations & Open Questions
- **Verified Methodological & Data Limitations:** (1) Sizes are small (L ≤ 32): bootstrap errors of the largest-pair crossings are {{num:cross_sq_24_32_bse}} (square) and {{num:cross_tri_24_32_bse}} (triangular), and pair crossings spread over {{num:tc_sq_spread_pct}} % and {{num:tc_tri_spread_pct}} % of T_c. (2) Robustness rules, the crossing selection and several diagnostics were fixed after the data were seen (Section 3). (3) Seven sources (C1, C2, C5, C6, C9, C11 and the uncited Selke paper) could be read only as abstracts or not at all, and the exact triangular T_c, Ising 1925 and Binder 1981 could not be retrieved in full text. The online searches and the local inventory are logged in `appendix/data_attempts.csv`, and the dependent claims are tagged `[Preliminary]`. (4) The test covers one universality class and cannot discriminate weak from strong emergence.
- **Actionable Next Steps / Experiments:** Extend to L = 64–128 with histogram reweighting and a fitted correction term; judge the 1D clause by an axis-independent criterion such as U₄ at fixed T as N grows; replace nearest-neighbour block correlation by a fitted renormalized coupling to locate the block-spin fixed point; and design an emergence test with an external benchmark that can fail, e.g. predicting a critical temperature before an independent determination.
