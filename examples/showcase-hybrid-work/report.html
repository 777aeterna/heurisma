Hybrid Work (2 Days Remote) vs. Full-Time Office in Software Engineering: Retention, Output, and Seniority Heterogeneity

As-Of Date: 2026-11-20 | Depth Level: Level 2 (Empirical / Data Study) | Primary Disciplines: Social Sciences & Economics (Labor Economics) + Engineering & Applied Sciences (Empirical Software Engineering)

Abstract

A six-month randomized trial reports a one-third lower quit rate under 2-day hybrid work (7.20% to 4.80%) [1] [moderate], and the supplied site file points the same way (12-month attrition 9.6% vs. 13.8%), but its allocation is undocumented, so it supports only [preliminary] claims [2], [3]. On output, the supplied sites show opposite-signed effects for senior and junior sites that an exact test separates from uniform effects (interaction p = 0.0004) [preliminary], and neither external study reports output by seniority.

Falsifier: A documented allocation protocol with pre-period baselines for the 16 sites, or engineer-level data controlling for defect severity, that removes the seniority interaction or reverses the attrition difference.

1. Executive Summary

  • What Would Change This Conclusion: A documented allocation protocol with pre-period baselines for the 16 sites, or engineer-level data controlling for defect severity, that removes the seniority interaction or reverses the attrition difference.
  • Key Takeaways:
    • Retention: the external evidence is a single randomized trial with no seniority split [1] [moderate]; the supplied sites agree in direction for both seniority groups, and the data cannot distinguish a smaller junior reduction from none (interaction p = 0.2959) [2], [3] [preliminary].
    • Output: pooling hides the divergence (pooled difference −1.78 pp, stratified exact p = 0.1804), while the seniority interaction is −9.00 pp [3] [preliminary].
    • Mechanism: reduced mentoring hours at junior hybrid sites are consistent with, but cannot test, a proximity mechanism supported elsewhere in a different setting [4] [preliminary].

2. Framing & Hypotheses

  • Scientific Discipline(s) & Depth Level: Social Sciences & Economics (Labor Economics — field experiments, retention) + Engineering & Applied Sciences (Empirical Software Engineering — pull-request throughput, mentoring) | Level 2. Labor Economics governs attrition identification and subgroup heterogeneity; Software Engineering governs the validity of the output metric and the mentoring bottleneck.
  • Research Question: Compared to a 5-day office schedule, does a 2-day/week hybrid policy change software-engineering PR output and 12-month retention, and is the effect the same for senior and junior engineers?
  • Hypotheses:
    • H₀: A 2-day hybrid schedule produces no change in PR output or 12-month attrition in any seniority group.
    • H₁: A 2-day hybrid schedule lowers attrition without changing output, and the output effect is the same across seniority groups.
    • H₂: A 2-day hybrid schedule lowers attrition overall, but its output effect differs by seniority.
  • Refutation Criteria: Because each seniority group has only four sites per policy, all tests are exact permutation tests (not bootstrap), with the threshold p < 0.05 fixed in advance. H₀ is falsified for retention if the stratified exact test of the attrition difference has p < 0.05. H₁ is falsified if the exact test of the seniority × policy interaction on PR output has p < 0.05. Rejecting H₁ does not by itself confirm any mechanism behind H₂.

4. Findings

4.1 Retention: External Trial Evidence and the Supplied Sites

The only randomized evidence is a six-month trial of 2-day hybrid work with 1,612 employees at Trip.com, assigned by birthday parity: the quit rate was 7.20% in the office group and 4.80% in the hybrid group (P = 0.043), a one-third reduction concentrated among non-managers, women, and long commuters, with no difference for managers [1] [moderate]. The trial reports no seniority split, and one company in one labor market over six months limits generalization [1].

In the supplied file, 12-month attrition averages 9.6% under hybrid and 13.8% under office when weighted by engineers (9.8% vs. 13.9% unweighted), a 30.1% relative reduction; the stratified exact test gives p = 0.0004 [2], [3] [preliminary]. The reduction appears in both groups, −5.28 pp for senior sites (p = 0.0286, relative 39.5%) and −2.75 pp for junior sites (p = 0.0286, relative 19.2%), where 0.0286 is the smallest p a 4-vs-4 comparison can reach. The junior reduction is smaller, but the exact interaction test cannot tell it from no difference (+2.53 pp, p = 0.2959) [3] [preliminary].

The two estimates are not combined into one range: the trial measures quits over six months in a travel-technology company, and the file reports 12-month attrition at engineering sites with undocumented allocation [2], [1].

4.2 Seniority Heterogeneity in Output Rejects Uniform Effects (H₁)

Pooling all sites gives a small difference (−0.74% hybrid vs. +1.04% office; −1.78 pp, stratified exact p = 0.1804), which conceals opposite-signed subgroup differences [3] [preliminary]:

SubgroupSitesHybrid (2d) PR DeltaOffice (5d) PR DeltaDifferenceExact p
Pooled16−0.74%+1.04%−1.78 pp0.1804
Senior8+2.97%+0.25%+2.73 pp0.0286
Junior8−4.45%+1.82%−6.28 pp0.0286

Within each seniority group, every hybrid site lies on one side of every office site, so each subgroup test sits at its floor (0.0286). The interaction test over all 4,900 stratified relabelings gives −9.00 pp (p = 0.0004), which rejects the uniform-effect hypothesis H₁ under the pre-specified threshold [3] [preliminary]. The output pattern is consistent with H₂, but this does not identify why it arises. The trial reports no evidence of an effect on performance grades or promotions overall [1] [moderate]; it does not report results by seniority.

4.3 Mentoring Hours: Consistent Pattern, Mechanism Untested

Mentoring hours per week fall from 5.15 at junior office sites to 1.73 at junior hybrid sites, and from 4.45 to 4.12 at senior sites [2], [3] [preliminary]. The pooled junior correlation with PR delta (r = +1.00) is not a mechanism test: it is driven by the policy arm, which determines both variables. Within each arm (four sites each) the correlation is positive in all four cells (junior hybrid +0.99, junior office +0.99, senior hybrid +0.99, senior office +0.68), which four points cannot support as evidence [3] [preliminary]. At senior sites, mentoring hours are slightly lower under hybrid while output is higher, so a single "mentoring drives output" mechanism does not fit both groups.

A quasi-experimental study of software engineers finds that sitting near teammates raises coding feedback by 18.3% and improves code quality, with gains concentrated among less-tenured and younger employees and experienced engineers writing less code nearby [4] [moderate]. That evidence concerns proximity within teams; the supplied file cannot test it, because each site is wholly senior or wholly junior and the direction of mentoring_hrs_wk (hours given or received) is undocumented [2].

5. Counter-Evidence & Alternative Explanations

  • Undocumented Allocation: Without a documented assignment of sites to policies and a pre-period baseline, differences between hybrid and office sites may reflect site selection, project complexity, or manager quality [2] [preliminary].
  • Survivorship and Composition: Output is measured on engineers who remain; office sites lose about four percentage points more engineers, so who is observed differs by arm, and the baseline window of the PR delta is undefined [2], [3] [preliminary].
  • PR Count Is an Incomplete Proxy: Merged pull requests do not capture quality, incident rates, or review load, and senior engineers may merge more partly because they review less junior code [4] [moderate].
  • External Validity of the Trial: One company, one country, hybrid-versus-office only, with the effect concentrated in specific subgroups and no seniority breakdown [1] [moderate].
  • Tiny Cells: Four sites per cell make the exact tests coarse and confidence intervals uninformative, so no interval is reported [3] [preliminary].

7. Limitations & Open Questions

  • Verified Methodological & Data Limitations: Site-level aggregates (n = 16 sites, 590 engineers, four per cell) with no documented allocation, baseline window, or data dictionary, and no engineer-level records (searches logged in appendix/data_attempts.csv); the trial's data availability statement and the proximity study's full text were not read. The QA gate records one accepted FAIL (appendix/gates/qa.md, check 3): the verdict's 7.20% and 4.80% are the external trial's published quit rates [1], not outputs of appendix/analysis.py.
  • Actionable Next Steps / Experiments: Request from the data owner the allocation protocol, pre-period attrition and output by site, the definition of mentoring_hrs_wk, and engineer-level records; re-estimate with pre-period baselines and attrition-adjusted bounds; and read the full text of the proximity study before relying on its subgroup magnitudes.

"""Reproducible Level 2 hybrid-work audit: exact stratified permutation tests on 16 sites (4 vs 4 per seniority cell).

With only four sites per cell a bootstrap is unreliable (35 distinct resample means per arm), so every test here is an
exact permutation test that enumerates all assignments: 70 per subgroup, 4,900 for stratified pooled/interaction tests.
Run from the report folder: python3 -B appendix/analysis.py --charts-out charts.json --numbers-out appendix/numbers.json
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import argparse
import json
import math
import statistics
import sys
from itertools import combinations, product
from pathlib import Path

ROOT = Path(__file__).resolve().parents[1]
SCRIPTS_DIR = ROOT.parents[1] / "skills" / "heurisma" / "scripts"
sys.path.insert(0, str(SCRIPTS_DIR))
from local_data import read_table  # noqa: E402

EPS = 1e-12


def pearson_r(xs: list[float], ys: list[float]) -> float:
    mx, my = statistics.fmean(xs), statistics.fmean(ys)
    num = sum((a - mx) * (b - my) for a, b in zip(xs, ys))
    den = math.sqrt(sum((a - mx) ** 2 for a in xs) * sum((b - my) ** 2 for b in ys))
    return num / den if den else 0.0


def split_diffs(a: list[float], b: list[float]) -> tuple[float, list[float]]:
    """Observed mean(a)-mean(b) and the same difference for every way of relabeling len(a) of the pooled sites as 'a'."""
    vals = a + b
    n, total = len(a), sum(vals)
    obs = statistics.fmean(a) - statistics.fmean(b)
    diffs = []
    for idx in combinations(range(len(vals)), n):
        sa = sum(vals[i] for i in idx)
        diffs.append(sa / n - (total - sa) / (len(vals) - n))
    return obs, diffs


def p_two_sided(obs: float, null: list[float]) -> float:
    return sum(1 for d in null if abs(d) >= abs(obs) - EPS) / len(null)


def weighted_mean(rows: list[dict], key: str) -> float:
    w = sum(int(r["n_engineers"]) for r in rows)
    return sum(float(r[key]) * int(r["n_engineers"]) for r in rows) / w


def cell(rows: list[dict], seniority: str, policy: str) -> list[dict]:
    return [r for r in rows if r["seniority"] == seniority and r["policy_group"] == policy]


def main() -> None:
    parser = argparse.ArgumentParser(description="Analyze hybrid_rct_sites.csv and emit charts.json + numbers.json.")
    parser.add_argument("--charts-out", type=Path, default=None, help="Output path for charts.json v2.")
    parser.add_argument("--numbers-out", type=Path, default=None, help="Output path for appendix/numbers.json.")
    args = parser.parse_args()

    rows = read_table(ROOT / "appendix" / "data" / "hybrid_rct_sites.csv")
    total_eng = sum(int(r["n_engineers"]) for r in rows)
    sh, so = cell(rows, "Senior", "Hybrid_2d"), cell(rows, "Senior", "Office_5d")
    jh, jo = cell(rows, "Junior", "Hybrid_2d"), cell(rows, "Junior", "Office_5d")
    hyb, off = sh + jh, so + jo

    def col(rs: list[dict], key: str) -> list[float]:
        return [float(r[key]) for r in rs]

    results: dict[str, float] = {}
    for key, label in (("prs_merged_delta_pct", "pr"), ("attrition_12m_pct", "attr")):
        d_s, null_s = split_diffs(col(sh, key), col(so, key))
        d_j, null_j = split_diffs(col(jh, key), col(jo, key))
        null_pool = [(x + y) / 2.0 for x, y in product(null_s, null_j)]
        null_did = [y - x for x, y in product(null_s, null_j)]
        results.update(
            {
                f"{label}_sen_diff": d_s,
                f"{label}_sen_p": p_two_sided(d_s, null_s),
                f"{label}_jun_diff": d_j,
                f"{label}_jun_p": p_two_sided(d_j, null_j),
                f"{label}_pool_diff": (d_s + d_j) / 2.0,
                f"{label}_pool_p": p_two_sided((d_s + d_j) / 2.0, null_pool),
                f"{label}_did": d_j - d_s,
                f"{label}_did_p": p_two_sided(d_j - d_s, null_did),
            }
        )
    n_sub, n_strat = len(split_diffs(col(sh, "n_engineers"), col(so, "n_engineers"))[1]), 0
    n_strat = n_sub * n_sub
    p_floor_sub = 2.0 / n_sub

    # Engineer-weighted attrition (descriptive) and relative drops
    aw_h, aw_o = weighted_mean(hyb, "attrition_12m_pct"), weighted_mean(off, "attrition_12m_pct")
    au_h, au_o = statistics.fmean(col(hyb, "attrition_12m_pct")), statistics.fmean(col(off, "attrition_12m_pct"))
