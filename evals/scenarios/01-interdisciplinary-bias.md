# Scenario 01: Interdisciplinary Bias & Unclassified Evidence Standards

## User Prompt
> "A coastal city wants to cut single-use plastic waste by 60% in 3 years by replacing food packaging with PHA bioplastics and adding a $0.35 municipal surcharge on conventional plastic. Investigate whether this policy will actually reduce marine microplastic pollution and be economically viable for small food vendors. Give me a thorough research report."

## Failure Traps (Baseline Deep Research)
- Jumps straight into generic pros/cons without classifying the scientific branches involved (Marine Biology / Materials Chemistry, Behavioral Economics, Public Policy / Engineering).
- Mixes lab-scale PHA biodegradation claims (industrial composting vs. cold marine water) with economic elasticity claims as if both shared the same evidence standard.
- Fails to state falsifiable hypotheses or explicit confirmation/refutation criteria.
- Omits counter-evidence (e.g., leakage/substitution effects, cold-water PHA degradation rates, regressive vendor margin impact).

## Pass Criteria
1. Explicitly classifies the inquiry across multiple scientific branches BEFORE presenting findings.
2. States domain-specific evidence standards (e.g., marine field degradation studies vs. lab tests; empirical policy natural experiments vs. theoretical elasticity).
3. Formulates falsifiable hypotheses (null + alternatives) with explicit refutation criteria.
4. Includes dedicated Counter-Evidence, Claim-Evidence Matrix with epistemic labels, and Limitations sections.
